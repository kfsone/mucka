## The knees, in detail

This was the claim the alarm model rests on, and it lands exactly.

Method: the FES heartbeat reports **effective** strength and dexterity, so the unknown base
(bonuses, carried weight, the per-object rounding term) cancels out of a *difference* between
two snapshots taken while the carried inventory is unchanged. Then a second, independent
cross-check reads the base directly off an above-knee snapshot - where the penalty is zero by
definition - and predicts the below-knee values with no fitting at all.

Observed, one continuous descent during a ram fight, base STR 97 / base DEX 104:

| stamina | eff STR | predicted | eff DEX | predicted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 44 | 97 | 97 | 104 | 104 |
| 42 | 97 | 97 | 104 | 104 |
| 33 | 97 | 97 | **102** | 104 - (40-33)/3 = 102 |
| 25 | **95** | 97 - (30-25)/2 = 95 | **99** | 104 - (40-25)/3 = 99 |
| 19 | **92** | 97 - (30-19)/2 = 92 | **97** | 104 - (40-19)/3 = 97 |

Every below-knee value is exact. A grid over thresholds {0,20,25,30,35,40,45,50} and divisors
{1,2,3,4} ranks `(30-S)/2` first for strength and `(40-S)/3` first for dexterity, with every
rival strictly worse. **As a STAT formula, a knee at 20 fits worse than no knee at all.**

> Read that narrowly. It says only that the engine does not change its strength or dexterity
> arithmetic at 20 stamina. It does **not** say 20 is unimportant - 20 is the survival threshold,
> where flee cost falls, where the game itself prompts you to consider fleeing, where a single blow
> from most creatures can kill, and where several creatures turn hostile. Outside rats, 3 of the
> owner's 5 visits to exactly 20 stamina cost the character. See `COMBAT-RAIL-SPEC.md` section 6a:
> the stat knees at 40 and 30 EXPLAIN, the threshold at 20 ALARMS. Concluding otherwise from this
> line would be reading an instrument as if it were an intent.

**Honest limits.** The above-knee half of this is overwhelming - 87 of the 90 stamina changes
moved effective strength not at all, which is what rules out any threshold higher than 30 or 40
(a knee at 45 or 50 would have fired on dozens of them). The below-knee half is
thin: 2 distinct sub-30 stamina levels and 3 sub-40 levels, from essentially one near-death
episode. Each lands on the exact integer out of a range where a wrong slope would visibly miss,
so it is evidence rather than coincidence, but **the slopes are the least-tested part** and one
more near-death fight would roughly double the sample.

A trap worth recording, because it nearly produced a false refutation: across the single flee in
this corpus the player's **base strength jumped 97 -> 105 and base dexterity 104 -> 105 while the
carried-inventory list read identical**. Anchoring a below-knee snapshot to an above-knee one
across that boundary makes the published formula look wrong by 8 points. The tool now requires
the anchor to share both the inventory *and* the score, and to sit within 20s.

## What the data adds that the document does not say

### 1. Mobiles pay the stamina knee too

The document states the strength knee for the player and never says mobiles are subject to it.
They are, and it is the only way the small creatures make sense.

A rat has 25 stamina, so it spends its *entire life* below the 30-stamina strength knee, and gets
weaker every time we hit it. Against the flat published bound its blows look impossibly feeble:

| creature | STR | flat bound `STR/6+1` | blows | observed max | P(max this low) | observed mean | flat mean | knee mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water-snakes | 60 | 11 | 36 | 11 | 1.00 | 5.64 | 6.0 | 5.79 |
| Rat1-21 | 30 | 6 | 15 | **4** | **0.002** | **2.40** | 3.5 | **2.70** |
| Ram0 | 80 | 14 | 11 | 12 | 0.18 | 6.91 | 7.5 | 7.5 |
| Zombie0-7 | 80 | 14 | 10 | 14 | 1.00 | 8.20 | 7.5 | 7.0 |
| Raven | 40 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 0.13 | 2.67 | 4.0 | 3.33 |

Fifteen rat blows with a maximum of 4 has probability 0.002 under a uniform 1..6. Apply the
published knee to the *attacker's* CS - estimating its remaining stamina as published STA minus
our running band-midpoint damage - and the rat's ceiling per blow becomes 3-5, its predicted mean
2.70 against 2.40 observed, and the anomaly disappears. Zero blows in the whole corpus exceed the
flat bound; one exceeds the tighter knee-adjusted bound.

This is a derived result, not a published one, and it matters for the client: **a wounded
creature hits softer as well as dying sooner.** Damage-taken forecasts that use flat published
STR will systematically over-warn against small creatures.

### 2. The FES *does* show the "invisible" dexterity halving

The document asserts (section 3, step 3) that `score` "shows your general effective dexterity and
cannot show the halving that applies only against an opponent you cannot see."

Our heartbeat shows it. On every one of 12 blind/unblind transitions across 4 separate episodes,
effective dexterity moves by exactly **60** with stamina and inventory held constant:

```
blind ram  ->  You have suddenly and magically gone blind!
FES:  ... 104 100 ...   ->   ... 44 100 ...        (dexterity 104 -> 44)
unblind me ->  You have suddenly and magically regained your sight!
FES:  ... 44 100 ...    ->   ... 104 100 ...
```

With base D = 100 that is `D/10 + D/2 = 10 + 50 = 60`: both step 2 (surroundings) and step 3
(target) firing together, and both visible in the readout. Caveat the sample cannot escape: base
D was 100 in every observation, so "60" is also just "a flat 60" - a character with a different
base dexterity would separate the two readings in one snapshot.

Practical consequence: a blind player's real hit chance against a rat drops from 100/175 = 57% to
40/175 = 23%. That is worth an alarm, and it is directly observable rather than inferred.

### 3. A failed enemy escape pays 4 points

Seven times in one fight, `The water-snake5 has fled by trying to go <dir>. / You can fight it no
longer.` was followed by a score increase of exactly **+4**. Nothing in the document mentions it.

The interaction is the interesting part. That same snake's kill award came in at **79** instead of
the standard 86 - short by exactly one point per failed escape. So the flee bonus is not bolted on
top; it looks like it comes out of the same budget, with the player netting 79 + 28 = 107 against
a clean 86.

### 4. Weapon strengths, fitted

MUD2 reports our damage only as a band, but the band is a censored observation of a uniform
`1..(CS/6)+1` and the FES gives effective strength at each blow, so weapon strength is the single
free parameter. Fitted by maximum likelihood, with 95% likelihood-ratio support intervals:

| weapon | blows | eff STR range | implied weapon STR | support | implied max damage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| falchion | 70 | 84-94 | 24 | 20-25 | 20 |
| axe0 | 40 | 91-97 | 21 | 18-28 | 20 |
| croquet mallet | 10 | 97-102 | 11 | 0-35 | 19 |

The falchion and axe0 are within noise of each other; the croquet mallet has almost no constraint
(10 blows, never seen in the top band). Treat these as order-of-magnitude until the guide's
weapons page is transcribed - at which point this table becomes a direct check on it.

### 5. The combat lattice is exactly 2.000 s

161 exchange gaps, every one an integer multiple of 2.000s (68 x 1, 46 x 2, 35 x 3, 12 x 4, zero
off-lattice). Arrival phase modulo 2000ms clusters at 145-374ms across a whole session, which is
receive jitter on a fixed server lattice, not a drifting period. Gaps longer than one period are
rounds where neither side acted - the "usually" in the published wording, governed by each
mobile's `Speed`.

## Where the FAQ is contradicted, plainly

1. **"score ... cannot show the halving that applies only against an opponent you cannot see."**
   False for the FES heartbeat. The full 60-point blind penalty is in the readout. (n=12
   transitions, 4 episodes - solid.)

2. **`Ram0` Points = 106.** Our one ram kill awarded **313**, with a clean FES bracket 2.9s wide,
   no other kill in it, and no treasure appearing in the room. Every other species matched its
   published `Points` to the point: zombies 38, rats 22, water-snakes 86, raven 34 - 19 exact
   matches out of 21. **n=1**, so this is a flag, not a refutation. Candidate explanations: the
   mobile was carrying value we never saw; the table's figure is a transcription error; roaming
   rams are not `Ram0`. A second ram kill settles it.

3. **"summed bracket midpoints approximate damage in stamina points"** (section 8) is right, but
   the section's own group medians are systematically *high* for small creatures - rats read 31.2
   against a published 25 - because one blow overshoots. The tool replaces the midpoint sum with
   a two-sided bracket: lower = summed band minima of every blow but the killing one (the creature
   was demonstrably alive), upper = summed band maxima of all blows. 22 of 23 kills put the
   published STA inside that bracket. That is a real constraint, and the honest way to state the
   result.

4. **Not a contradiction, but the guide's zombie regeneration is load-bearing and missing from
   `bestiary.tsv`.** The single pool miss is `zombie7`: 6 blows whose minima sum to 52 against a
   published pool of 40, over a 26s (about 13-round) fight. Implied regeneration 0.92/round - and
   the guide's own worked example credits a zombie with 1 regen/round. `bestiary.tsv` has no
   regeneration column, so this is a reconciliation inferred from the discrepancy it explains, not
   a confirmation. **Transcribing the guide's regeneration figures would turn the pool test from
   INCONCLUSIVE into a real test**, and would matter for the client: a creature that outheals a
   weak attacker is a fight you should not start.

5. **Flee cost: one measurement, and the interesting question is not the one asked.** Fled at 19/105
   stamina and lost **2079 points from 46416** - 4.48% of score.

   An earlier draft of this section called that a disagreement with the owner's remembered flee of
   1300 from 13000. **That comparison was invalid**: the two flees happened at different stamina on
   what the owner describes as a steep curve, so they are two points on a slope rather than two
   estimates of one quantity. Retracted.

   The owner's model, from play: maximum loss is **10% of current score, flat down to 20 stamina**,
   dropping quickly below that, roughly 1.2% at 7 stamina, and **free below about 6**. Our single
   measurement sits neatly on it - 4.48% at 19, against a 10% maximum of ~4642 - and it means
   **fleeing at 20 costs more than twice fleeing at 19**, a cliff sitting exactly on the survival
   threshold.

   The real open question is **whether the bands key on absolute stamina or on fraction of maximum**.
   The guide says percentages; the owner's thresholds are absolute; on a 105-stamina character the
   two nearly coincide, and for a 30-stamina character they differ enormously. Nothing in this
   corpus can separate them - it needs a flee on a character with a very different maximum stamina.
   Verdict stays INSUFFICIENT.

   What this run does establish, narrowly: fleeing at 19 stamina was *not* free.

## Sampling gaps worth closing

Ordered by how much they would buy.

1. **A second flee, in a different band.** Turns the only INSUFFICIENT-with-real-stakes verdict
   into a testable ladder. Two flees in different bands pin the base rate; three settle it.
2. **A periodic `score` alongside the FES heartbeat.** `weight_carried_grams`, `objects_carried`,
   `raw_strength` and `raw_dexterity` are NULL for all 1429 snapshots because they come only from
   a `score` text parse. That single change unlocks steps 1-3 of the effective-strength formula -
   the weight, weight-squared and per-object-rounding terms - which are currently untestable.
3. **One more near-death fight.** Doubles the below-knee sample and moves the knee *slopes* from
   "exact but thin" to settled.
4. **A second ram kill**, to resolve the 313-vs-106 points anomaly.
5. **A deliberately idle capture** - no combat, no sleeping, no drinking, dense FES polling - to
   separate the regeneration tick period from the amount regained per tick. Note for whoever does
   it: MUD2 **stops answering the FES poll while you sleep**, so a nap reads as a single enormous
   heal. The tool discards any window overlapping a `sleep` command for exactly this reason.
6. **A character with base dexterity other than 100**, which would separate `D/10 + D/2` from
   "a flat 60" in a single snapshot.
7. **Attack a sleeping mobile**, with the same weapon used against it awake, to test the 100%
   hit / +50% damage claim.

## Method notes that changed a result

- **Fight scoping.** Pool damage is accumulated per `(capture, instance name)` across fights, not
  per fight. The ram was fled from once and finished later; scoped to the killing fight alone it
  reads 36-65 against a published 100 (a false MISS), and across both fights 82-138 (consistent).
  Keying on capture matters - the same name after a reset is a different creature at full health.
- **Pack fights and the score bracket.** Two rats can die in the same instant between one FES poll
  and the next, and the score delta then covers both awards. Those brackets are dropped rather
  than reported as a doubled award.
- **NPC damage attribution.** `The ram hits you (57/105)` reports stamina *after* the blow, so
  damage is the drop from the last known reading - and both FES snapshots and *earlier hit lines*
  update that reading. Attributing every blow in a round to the pre-round FES triple-counts in a
  rat pack; an earlier draft of this analysis did exactly that and reported a rat maximum of 6.
